# AGORA Decision Cell — Design Spec (DRAFT v0.3)

Status: DRAFT for final owner review. Docs only. Approves no code, no engine edit (`agora/sim/**`), no data, no feed, no run, no flag change. `VALIDATED_PREDICTION_ENABLED` stays False.

## 1. Purpose
When something new happens (breaking news, a viral video, a leak report, an incident), a named human in charge can bring it into a running or finished study and choose, with full information, how AGORA should respond. The system never acts on its own: it verifies, shows, runs what the human chooses, and records everything.

Non-goals: predicting that an event will occur; autonomous action; targeting or directing force; using private or leaked material as a source; any claim of validated prediction.

## 2. The loop
1. **Intake** — event items from feeds or typed by people. Each carries source, time received, time of event, and who submitted it.
2. **Verify** — dedupe; source trust score; label each statement CLAIM, REPORTED-BY-MULTIPLE, or CONFIRMED (definitions in section 2A). Unconfirmed items can raise a flag but cannot change a forecast without a human choice.
3. **Situation view** — plain words: known, unknown, changed since last view, which studies and segments are touched.
4. **Human choice** (section 3).
5. **Run** — the chosen path executes in the governed pipeline; every input is hash-linked.
6. **Result** — ranges by segment and branch, with the event and its confidence shown beside them.
7. **After-action** — later, predicted versus observed is recorded; hit and false-alarm rates are tracked per source and event type.

## 2A. Verification definitions
- **Source independence.** Two sources are independent only if neither cites, syndicates or copies the other and they do not share an owner or a single upstream origin (wire service, press release, one original post). Independence is judged per statement and recorded; if it cannot be established the sources count as one.
- **Minimum evidence.** REPORTED-BY-MULTIPLE needs at least two independent sources for the same statement. CONFIRMED needs either a primary source (the party itself, an official record, original footage with provenance) or at least two independent sources of which one is primary or has a published correction policy.
- **Event time.** Each item carries event-time (when it happened), received-time (when we got it) and an event-time uncertainty (exact, minutes, hours, day, unknown). An item with unknown event time cannot be injected at a specific round; the human must pick the round and that choice is logged.
- **Timezone and market close.** All times are stored in UTC with the source's original local time and zone kept alongside. The company client's business time zone is set per project (default Asia/Kolkata). Where an event is tied to a market, the project record names the market calendar; an event after that market's close (or on a non-trading day) is assigned to the next session open for outcome windows, and the rule applied is logged. Ambiguous local times (daylight-saving overlap) are refused and sent to the human.
- **Auditable source trust.** The trust score is a recorded function of named, versioned inputs (source class, track record from the after-action hit and false-alarm table, correction history, independence group) and is shown with its inputs; each score and each change is audit-logged with the version of the rule used. The primary-source CONFIRMED exception requires the approver to record which primary source and why it qualifies; the exception is flagged in every result that relies on it and counted in review reports.
- **Corrections and retractions.** A retraction or correction is a new item linked to the original. Anything run on a retracted item is stamped "based on retracted item" in every result and report; the human is shown which branches are affected and can fork without it.
- **Who may mark CONFIRMED.** The designated project owner, identified in the project record, is the approver and may promote an item to CONFIRMED with a reason text. No delegate is enabled by default. A delegate may act only after the owner records that person's exact identity and project scope in the project record. The system may propose, never promote. Promotion and demotion are audit-logged.

**Implementation status (library only, 27 Nov 2026):** `agora/decision_cell/verification.py` covers, for one statement, source independence, the minimum-evidence rules and who may mark CONFIRMED. The system computes only CLAIM or REPORTED-BY-MULTIPLE. Promotion and demotion need the recorded owner or a recorded delegate holding the high-assurance approver role; both, and refused attempts, are recorded in the anchored audit chain. A primary-source-only confirmation must name the source and a reason, and its result carries the exception flag. Choices made here: a pair is independent only with a recorded judgment for that statement, no contrary judgment, known owners and origins for both and for every listed source they draw on, no owner in common across those two sets, and no shared upstream in the supplied citations and origins, both followed through every listed source they reach, and no identical content hash among them; if a qualifying independent pair exists the confirmation rests on it and no exception is claimed; a promotion applies only to the evidence judged: the statement wording, source attributes, per-source content hashes and judgments (any change returns the computed label, marked stale) and must be demoted before a new one. Limits: source attributes, content hashes and judgments are trusted service-layer inputs that nothing here authenticates or checks against any content, and hidden copying is not detected; after a wording change, earlier judgments still count towards the computed label; the chain keeps hashes of the evidence and reasons, not the text. **Not built:** event intake, dedupe, event time and time zones, source trust scores, retractions, showing the flag in results or counting it in reports, and any service, command or screen. Update (28 Nov 2026): `request_labelled_option` binds these labels into the option gate (section 3A); the older gate calls do not read them.

**Retractions and corrections (library only, 28 Nov 2026):** `agora/decision_cell/retractions.py` records a new item as the retraction or correction of an original in the anchored audit chain. This replaces "retractions" in the not-built list above. Choices made here: recording needs the same person as CONFIRMED (this spec named none); both items need a recorded statement list, and the new item's must be the later one, which rules out cycles; one new item links to one original; a link is never withdrawn, and retracting a retraction does not clear the first stamp. A link forbids no option. It is part of the item's evidence view, so a deciding choice must be made on a view that includes it, an earlier second approval does not cover that choice, and a choice or window answer is not recorded if a link arrives between the read and the commit. `retraction_stamps` returns "based on retracted item" for each retracted item among those a caller names, and `allowed_run_attempts` lists the run choices the gate allowed on an item, marking those made before its first link. Limits: nothing checks that the new item really retracts or corrects anything; no result store, report or screen calls the stamp, and nothing records which items a result rests on; a gate pass is not proof that a run happened; attempts made before the item had a statement list, or on another item using it as a CHANGE_INPUTS input, are not listed. **Not built:** showing affected branches and forking without the item (there is no branch or result store here).

**What a run result rests on (library only, 28 Nov 2026):** `agora/decision_cell/run_results.py` records, once, the items a run result rests on, in the anchored audit chain. This replaces "nothing records which items a result rests on" above. `run_result_stamps` then needs only the result id, and applies retractions recorded before or after the result; `run_results_resting_on` lists the recorded results that include an item. Choices made here: a result is recorded against one allowed, labelled run choice, whose item is always in the basis; the caller adds other items and parent results, and a parent's whole basis is copied into the child; every item must have a recorded statement list; a basis is never changed; at most 200 items and 20 parents, refused rather than cut. Where items cannot be told apart it stamps rather than clears: a fork's without-event branch, or a CHANGE_INPUTS run that left the choice's own item out, keeps that item, so an unstamped rerun has to come from a choice on another item with no parent that used the retracted one. Limits: recording is the trusted service's record, with no person permission check; nothing checks that a run happened, that the result hash matches any output, or that the named items and parents are complete (CHANGE_INPUTS inputs are kept only as hashes); an unrecorded result is refused by the stamp call and absent from the list. No engine, report or screen calls it. **Not built:** the executor that would call it, showing branches, and forking without the item.

**Executing a fork from the event (library only, 28 Nov 2026):** `agora/decision_cell/executor.py` is the first executor; the design note is [DECISION_CELL_EXECUTOR.md](DECISION_CELL_EXECUTOR.md). It replaces "the executor that would call it" and "forking without the item" in the not-built list above, for FORK_FROM_EVENT only. No service, command or screen calls it, it runs the mock backend only, and showing branches is still not built. Update (28 Nov 2026): it also executes CONTINUE (`execute_continuation`), as one branch restored from the last committed round into a new project, with no baseline. Second update (28 Nov 2026): it also executes RERUN_FROM_START (`execute_rerun`) as a new project run from round 0, with the statements added after the event round the person picks; there is no recorded event time, so "from its real timestamp" is only as right as that choice. Third update (28 Nov 2026): it also executes FORK_FROM_EARLIER (`execute_earlier_fork`): both branches start from the chosen earlier snapshot and the statements are added in the round after it, recorded as a hypothetical earlier arrival; no different response is modelled. Fourth update (28 Nov 2026): it also executes one case of CHANGE_INPUTS (`execute_changed_inputs_fork`): a fork from the event with some, not all, of one item's recorded statements added. Choosing by source or trust level, across items or over another run is not built, and statement sensitivity is not recorded, so the data-owner grant check rests on the trusted service and the other executable options do not make it.

## 3. Human options (shown to everyone with access; executable only if permitted, see 3A)
| Option | What it does |
|---|---|
| IGNORE | Log the event, leave the study unchanged, record why. |
| HOLD | Defer with a re-check time; the forecast is stamped "unreviewed event pending". |
| CONTINUE (inject) | Keep agents' state; add the event at the next open round; finish the run. Fast. |
| FORK FROM EVENT | Copy the study at the event round; run branch A (with event) and branch B (without); compare. |
| FORK FROM ANY EARLIER ROUND | Same, from a chosen earlier snapshot, e.g. to test "what if we had responded sooner". |
| RERUN FROM START | Full rerun with the event present from its real timestamp; slowest, cleanest. |
| CHANGE INPUTS | Choose which evidence to include or exclude (per item, per source, per trust level) and rerun or fork with that set. |
| COMPARE | Side-by-side of any branches already run. |
| ESCALATE | Send to a second named approver before anything runs (for sensitive projects). |
Options can be combined (e.g. fork from event with a different evidence set). Each choice needs: the person's identity, a reason text, and the time budget. A choice that costs more than the budget asks for confirmation.\n\n**Suggested-action card (non-executing):** For each typed event, show one suggested next step with the evidence and trust labels behind it, why it is suggested, affected studies, expected cost, and any unmet permission/snapshot/budget gates. Show the other human options beside it. The suggestion is advisory: it cannot promote an item, change a forecast, start a run, or publish a result. A permitted human must explicitly choose and confirm an executable action; blocked choices remain disabled with the failing reason.

Timeout rule: each project has a 15-minute response window. If nobody responds within that window, the system **holds the last human-approved result** and alerts the designated project owner. If the owner has explicitly named a backup contact in the project record, that contact is alerted too; no backup is inferred. It publishes nothing new and changes no forecast. If no approved result exists, nothing is published and the item stays pending. The hold, the alert and the elapsed time are audit-logged.

**Implementation status (library only):** `agora/decision_cell/response_window.py` opens a window only for an item with a recorded statement list (3A), and records the window, the hold, the elapsed time and who is owed an alert in the anchored audit chain. Only an allowed IGNORE, HOLD or run choice answers a window; a refused attempt, COMPARE and ESCALATE do not stop the clock, and a late answer is recorded after the hold. A HOLD must carry a timezone-aware re-check time, after now and at most 7 days ahead (the 7-day limit is an implementation choice; this spec named none). The time is bound into the gate's action fingerprint and stored with the decision. Only a HOLD can be followed by another decision. If its re-check time arrives first, the sweep, or the next response, records that a new alert is due to the owner and any named backup. `unreviewed_items` lists the study's items that have no decision other than HOLD; while it is not empty, whoever shows the forecast must stamp it "unreviewed event pending". Limits: nothing applies that stamp yet; a recorded run choice clears it although this module cannot know the run happened; HOLD is a member option, so a member can defer again each time, and each deferral is audit-logged without a new alert. The project record can name one backup contact, who is alerted and gains no authority. **Not built:** alert delivery, a timer that calls the sweep, a trusted time source, event intake, and any service, command or screen that uses this (including one that shows the stamp).

## 3A. Permission, snapshot and budget matrix
Showing an option is not the same as being able to run it. An option is executable only when all three checks pass; otherwise it is displayed greyed with the failing reason and the attempt is logged.

| Option | Permission needed | Snapshot needed | Budget check |
|---|---|---|---|
| IGNORE, HOLD, COMPARE | project member | none | none |
| CONTINUE (inject) | approver | current-round snapshot | cost estimate vs budget |
| FORK FROM EVENT | approver | snapshot at event round | cost estimate x2 branches |
| FORK FROM ANY EARLIER ROUND | approver | snapshot for that round must exist and verify | cost estimate x2 branches |
| RERUN FROM START | approver | none (full rerun) | full-run cost; second confirmation above budget |
| CHANGE INPUTS | approver; sensitive inputs need the data owner's grant | as for the underlying run | as for the underlying run |
| ESCALATE | project member | none | none; blocks other options until the second approver answers |

Rules: no snapshot means the option is shown as unavailable, not silently replaced by a rerun. Budget exceedance requires an explicit confirmation by the approver, recorded with the amount. Permissions come from the Principal and policy path already in AGORA; nothing here widens them.

**Implementation status (26 Nov 2026):** `agora/decision_cell/` implements only the gate for this matrix: permission, snapshot-probe, budget, sensitive-input grant, escalation, second approval, kill switch, and an audit entry for allowed and blocked attempts. The gate runs no option and has no UI, intake or suggested-action card; the response window is a separate library module that calls the gate, and verification labels (2A) reach it only through the labelled path below. Choices made here: a second approval covers one exact action; a rejected escalation blocks approver options; a pending one blocks every option; cost is counted in agent-rounds, two branches for a fork; over budget, the confirmed amount must equal the cost. Limits: the project record, study state, escalation, grants, input sensitivity, cost estimate and snapshot probe are trusted service-layer inputs that the gate cannot authenticate, and no service layer supplies them yet. The audit chain keeps hashes of the action and reason text, not the text, amounts other than the cost, or the identity of a snapshot. A gate result is not an authorization token.

**Labels in the gate (library only, 28 Nov 2026):** `agora/decision_cell/evidence.py` reads the label in force for each statement of an item and hashes that view. On its path (`request_labelled_option`, and `respond_to_item` when given the item's statements), IGNORE and the run options, CHANGE INPUTS included, must name the view the person was shown and are blocked if it has changed; HOLD, COMPARE and ESCALATE are not blocked by an old view. The view hash is part of the action fingerprint, so a second approval for one view does not cover another. The attempt records each statement's label, any stale confirmation and any primary-source exception. Nothing is recorded if a label or the project record changes before the commit; the window's answer is also refused if a label changes after the gate entry and before it (that gate entry stays). A label never allows or forbids an option by itself. Limits: the sources and judgments are trusted service-layer inputs; a matching hash does not show a person read the view; after the window answer is checked, a project-record change before it is stored is not detected; no report counts the exception flags and nothing shows them.

**Recorded statement list (library only, 28 Nov 2026):** `agora/decision_cell/statements.py` records each item's statement ids and a hash of each wording as versions in the anchored audit chain. A later version must keep every earlier statement as worded and add at least one; a corrected wording is a new statement. The labelled path requires this list and accepts exactly its statements. Once an item has one, `request_option`, `request_project_option` and `respond_to_item` without statements block IGNORE and the run options, CHANGE INPUTS included (`item_labels_required`); HOLD, COMPARE and ESCALATE remain available. An unlabelled choice is not committed, and a window is not answered, if a list appears first; a labelled choice is not committed if the list grows. The gate also refuses a padded or unprintable item id. Update (28 Nov 2026): `open_response_window` refuses an item with no recorded list (`NO_STATEMENT_LIST`), so an item windowed from this change on cannot be decided without labels, by the window or by a direct gate call. The refusal also means no clock runs and no alert becomes due: the intake service must record the list first and show a refusal to a person. Windows opened before the change stay readable and are not retrofitted. Limits: the list is the trusted service's record, written without a person's permission check and not checked against any content, so a statement left out of every version is never seen; an item that was never registered gets no window, but a direct gate call can still decide it without labels. **Not built:** event intake that would register items, and any service, command or screen.

**Stored project record (26 Nov 2026):** `agora/decision_cell/project_store.py` now supplies one of those trusted inputs, the project record. Choices made here: the first version is written by a high-assurance approver of the project who names themself as owner (first write wins); only that owner can write later versions; the owner cannot be changed (transfer is not built); versions are append-only and each is committed with an anchored audit event carrying its hash; reads refuse if any stored version differs from the chain; refused writes are audited; `request_project_option` takes the record from the store and puts its hash in the attempt's audit entry. Not built: backup contact, business time zone, market calendar, a service or UI that calls it, and suppliers for the other trusted inputs. Limits: the anchor is a local sidecar, and first-write ownership relies on the identity layer granting membership and the approver role with care.

## 4. Data the human can use
Anything already approved and bound for the study: the study's own sources, the governed package, previous branches, and event items that passed section 2. New feeds and new datasets need separate named owner approval. Personal data stays excluded unless a consent review passes.

## 5. Engine snapshot and replay requirement

- **Committed-round, full-state snapshots.** Capture only after a committed round. Bind all state that can affect future output, not only agent attributes: the run database schema and rows; post IDs and AUTOINCREMENT counters; follows, memberships and mutes; poll and conversion state; event queues; simulation clock, round and RNG state; graph state when present; and the exact LLM reply cache or deterministic response transcript.
- **Identity and integrity.** Bind the snapshot to scenario, seed, clock, event-set identity, model/backend/configuration, Agora source revision and runtime identity. Store a schema/version, canonical metadata digest and full state/cache/graph digests. Verify these before reuse or restore; write snapshots once and preserve incomplete or corrupt artifacts while refusing them.
- **Deterministic replay or refusal.** Fork only when the exact snapshot and response cache/transcript verify and the selected backend can replay deterministically. The current implementation refuses live/non-mock backends. Any unsupported, incomplete, mismatched or nondeterministic state is refused before the source run is changed.
- **Source-run immutability and provenance.** A fork creates a separate project, leaves its source run untouched, and records the parent snapshot digest and configuration.
- Estimate cost before a run (rounds remaining × agents) and show it to the human.

## 6. Governance
Default deny stays. Every choice, input and result is written to the audit log and linked to its source. Kill switch per study. Sensitive deployments (defence, security) are limited to decision support: situation awareness, scenario ranges, logistics impact. No targeting, no direction of force.

## 7. Validation (preregistered before any claim)
- **Stage A, event study on existing data.** Preregistered before any look at the outcome windows: (a) the dated event list, written from public record; ChatGPT's review is advisory only and is not owner approval (the owner approves and freezes the list); (b) for each event, the expected direction of the move (up, down, none) and the affected segment, fixed and hashed before the comparison; (c) cutoffs: the baseline is the last state average on or before the event date, outcome windows are 7 and 14 days after, and no data after the window is used; (d) overlap rule: events whose 14-day windows overlap are grouped into one cluster and scored once, not counted separately; windows that contain a later listed event are flagged; (e) hit means observed move has the preregistered sign and exceeds the preregistered minimum size; the size threshold is fixed with the list. Report all hits, misses and losses. Uses data already approved; no new data.
- **Stage B, replay:** archived news streams replayed in time order with no look-ahead; measure alert hit rate, false-alarm rate, time to alert.
- **Stage C, confirmatory:** a held-out cycle (2020 planned) under a new protocol with the owner's freeze sentence.
Any tie or loss is a valid result.
